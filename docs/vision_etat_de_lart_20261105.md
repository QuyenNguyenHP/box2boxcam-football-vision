# Vision for PitchLens: state of the art and priority experiments

Review (5 November 2026) of approaches that could help an iPhone on a gimbal film an amateur match, and the order in which to test them on our own footage. The final criterion is the time the **action stays well framed**, with smooth movement and few departures onto a false ball. Published performance helps choose experiments; the PitchLens numbers below come from experiments already done.

## Jev can choose a camera action

In the proposed PitchLens version, Jev replaces YOLO and directly chooses the framing from the latest images and, if useful, the iPhone orientation. The instruction could be "You are filming the match. Keep the main action in frame and avoid unnecessary movement." A `Choice` question offers left, right, stay and uncertain. DockKit executes the motion at the allowed speed. The Doom demo illustrates this decision loop with structured game state. [Presentation and Doom demo](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) · [Images documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/images)

The vision side accepts a sequence of timestamped JPEG or PNG images. Allowed dimensions are **280 to 1,568 pixels per side**, so our 1,920 x 1,080 images must be cropped or resized. The `Box` primitive can localize a target if a finer instruction is explored later. TypeSafe classes images and `Box` as experimental. [Image limits and usage](https://docs.typesafe.ai/images#limits) · [Box primitive](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/box)

Over 90 minutes at five decisions per second, that is 27,000 requests. At 0.042 USD per million input tokens, the cost is **2.27 USD at 2,000 tokens per request**, or **5.67 USD at 5,000 tokens**. At two decisions per second these scenarios cost **0.91 USD** and **2.27 USD**. These token counts are assumptions for the full request; to get the price of the chosen format, read `usage.input_tokens` on a pilot. [TypeSafe pricing](https://help.typesafe.ai/articles/5187202169-how-much-does-jev-cost) · [Image token counting](https://docs.typesafe.ai/images#image-tokens)

On 107 images from the two recordings, encoded as JPEG quality 80, median size is **80 KB at 960 x 540** and **140 KB at 1,280 x 720**. A request with three 80 KB images takes about **192 ms for upload alone** at 10 Mbit/s upstream, excluding headers and network variance. At five requests per second that is about 9.6 Mbit/s and 6.5 GB per match; at two per second, about 3.8 Mbit/s and 2.6 GB. The full delay adds processing and return, then the engine's response. TypeSafe quotes 70 to 500 ms on its general examples. End-to-end median and p95 must be measured on vision requests over the connection used at the stadium. [TypeSafe published measurements](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)

## Image and video: what each family can bring

| Need | Approach to look at | PitchLens experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live detection | **YOLO26 Nano / Small**. Specialized detector, Core ML export already in our chain. [YOLO26](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo26) · [Core ML export](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml) | Measure Nano 960 and Small 960 on the same field images and on the same iPhone while recording. Keep Nano 1280 as a control for the resolution choice. |
| Seeing small balls | **Cropping the source image or tiling**. SAHI formalizes detection on image pieces. [SAHI paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06934) | On a 1,920-pixel-wide image, an 8-pixel ball falls to about 4 pixels when the full width is brought to 960. A native 960 crop around the likely zone keeps its 8 pixels. Try a global view to find the action, then a local crop once we know where to look. |
| Linking detections over time | **Association with motion and uncertainty**, inspired by ByteTrack. A weak candidate can be kept if it fits the current track. [ByteTrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06864) | The Swift tracker already estimates image velocity and filters jumps. Add camera motion to this estimate, then measure target switches and losses. For this small fast object, distance to the predicted center is often a more practical signal than strict overlap of two successive boxes. |
| Recovering a blurred or faint ball | **Specialized temporal network**, inspired by TrackNet. It outputs a center-probability map from several images. TrackNetV4 also uses frame differences and was tested on tennis and badminton. BlurBall explicitly studies the blur trace in table tennis. [TrackNetV4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14543) · [BlurBall](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/CVsports/html/Gossard_BlurBall_Joint_Ball_and_Motion_Blur_Estimation_for_Table_Tennis_CVPRW_2026_paper.html) | Research candidate for shots and passes. Train on short annotated iPhone sequences, with causal history and camera compensation. Evaluate reacquisition and false trajectories as much as point accuracy. |
| Propagating an annotation or finding landmarks | **SAM 3.1** (prompted segmentation and tracking) and **CoTracker3** (point tracking). [SAM 3.1](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3/blob/main/RELEASE_SAM3p1.md) · [CoTracker3](https://github.com/facebookresearch/co-tracker) | Test SAM to extend offline a well-visible box over a short passage, CoTracker for fixed lines or points that help estimate rotation. Check drift on a ball of a few pixels or an occluded one. |
| Understanding what is being played | **Jev** for closed choices, **Qwen3-VL** as a multimodal comparator with localization and video understanding. **DINOv3** provides image features to which a small specialized head can be added. [Qwen3-VL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21631) · [DINOv3](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/dinov3/) | Classify a track as active ball, spare or doubt. For data collection, compare proposals and send disagreements to human review. A general video-question benchmark does not measure recall on tiny balls. |
| Anticipating framing | **Predictive control** and, for research, **V-JEPA 2 / 2.1**. Meta demonstrated a variant that predicts a future state from learned commands on robot arms. [V-JEPA 2-AC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1#S4) · [V-JEPA 2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482) | The first framing experiment can simulate rotations with existing geometry and trajectory. A V-JEPA route would learn to predict the action zone from player video. A version conditioned on gimbal commands would need synchronized observations and motions from that system. |

Camera geometry is a topic of its own. When the gimbal turns, players, scenery and ball all move in the image. This component can be estimated with the gyroscope, fixed landmarks and camera parameters, then ball velocity computed in a stabilized frame. Systems such as BroadTrack use field lines to estimate broadcast camera parameters. This is a path to more advanced framing, with a difficulty specific to our case when lines leave the image. [Core Motion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion) · [BroadTrack](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Magera_BroadTrack_Broadcast_Camera_Tracking_for_Soccer_WACV_2025_paper.pdf)

If the camera is to learn to "play", imitation is also a concrete route: hand-frame a few wide clips in the simulator and record the motions as examples. Reinforcement learning could then try motions in that simulator with a visibility and comfort score. Result quality depends on what is rewarded; for instance, rewarding only centering would make the camera correct every small deviation even when the action is already well framed. Work on sports framing has used human demonstrations, including on football matches. [Learning Camera Control in Dynamic Scenes from Limited Demonstrations](https://diglib7.eg.org/items/e6c4f8fc-d76f-424f-ae7e-cbc92013b809)

Capture deserves its own experiment. A shorter exposure can preserve the ball during a shot but needs more gain at night, and gain makes the image noisier. Comparing two settings on repeatable sequences, with the same resolution and model, shows whether the detector really benefits. [AVFoundation exposure](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/setexposuremodecustom(duration:iso:completionhandler:)) · [AVFoundation ISO](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/iso)

## Order of experiments on our videos

1. **Fix a field reference.** The 14 October set has 568 frames at about 2 Hz from a single reserved match. As of 5 November, 290 frames had been reviewed: 250 usable and 40 excluded. The 250 can give a first diagnosis on this subset, with possible bias from review order. To evaluate framing and brief losses, also annotate a few short passages more densely, with the active ball and its visibility state. To confirm a general improvement, keep another match separate. [Field protocol](field_validation_20261021.md)
2. **Measure the models already trained.** On the old validation at a 25% threshold, Nano 960 gets 95 true detections and 23 false, Small 960 gets 101 and 21, Nano 1280 gets 92 and 31. Small goes from 4 to 8 false detections on negative images. This justifies testing Small first, without concluding on the night field. Compare recall by size in the source image, false positives per minute, useful frame rate, p95 delay and stability after several minutes of recording. Mac replay results and GPU training results are distinct measures. [Nano / Small comparison](mobile_size_comparison_20261015.md) · [On-device Core ML measurement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreml/analyzing-a-core-ml-model-s-performance-in-xcode)
3. **Isolate the gain from tracking, then from framing.** With identical detections, compare the current policy with rotation compensation. Then compare the current recentering with a planner that simulates a few rotation speeds over 300 to 500 ms, favors a visible ball with margin and penalizes accelerations. The existing backtest reruns inference in the simulated frame, from the pixels available in the video. A wide, stable source makes this comparison clearer; a video where the camera already moved imposes the limits of the recorded field. Framing measures must use reference positions independent of the algorithm's predictions.
4. **Test Jev alone as cameraman.** Start at two decisions per second in a fairly wide shot, with the recent image and a short history of already-captured images. Replay decisions in the backtest with their real delay. On ordinary and difficult sequences, measure the time the action stays in frame, unnecessary departures, capture-to-command delay and cost. Compare with the current tracking under the same conditions. Vision alone should be able to decide to search, wait or follow play when the ball is too small to localize.

SoccerNet provides references for tracking players, recognizing ball actions or reconstructing game state from video. To learn context, it is a source of methods and data to explore. To choose an iPhone model, the decisive result remains testing on another amateur match filmed under PitchLens conditions.
